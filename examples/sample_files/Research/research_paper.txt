The Impact of Remote Work on Employee Productivity: A Meta-Analysis

Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive meta-analysis of 47 studies conducted between 2020-2024 examining the relationship between remote work arrangements and employee productivity. Our analysis reveals a nuanced picture with significant moderating factors.

1. Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated the adoption of remote work globally. Understanding its impact on productivity has important implications for organizational policy and workforce management.

2. Methodology

We conducted a systematic review following PRISMA guidelines. Inclusion criteria:
- Peer-reviewed studies published 2020-2024
- Sample size > 100 participants
- Quantitative productivity measures
- Full-time employees only

3. Results

Overall effect size: d = 0.23 (95% CI: 0.15-0.31), indicating a small positive effect of remote work on productivity.

Key moderating factors:
- Job autonomy: Higher autonomy associated with greater productivity gains
- Dependent care responsibilities: Negative moderator for productivity
- Technical infrastructure: Strong positive moderator
- Management trust: Significant positive moderator

4. Discussion

Our findings suggest remote work can enhance productivity under specific conditions. Organizations should invest in technology infrastructure and manager training to maximize benefits.

5. Limitations

Self-report bias, varying productivity definitions across studies, and limited longitudinal data constrain generalizability.
